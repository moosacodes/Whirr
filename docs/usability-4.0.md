# Whirr Bench 4.0 — five-person comprehension protocol

**Status: NOT RUN.** No participants have been recruited, no results are recorded, and no claim of usability validation is made. This is the protocol to execute, not evidence from it.

## Why this protocol

The 4.0 redesign changed the vocabulary from “shelf / live session / receipt” to “bench / sound tag / Listening Room / running minutes.” We need to know whether a first-time visitor understands the promise **and its limit** without a presenter.

The critical failure is not “did they like the colours.” It is whether they can answer:

1. What does Whirr do?
2. When does billing begin?
3. When does billing pause or stay free?
4. How is the price calculated?
5. What does the proof establish, and what does it not?
6. What happens when the model is uncertain?
7. How would a dispute work?

## Participants

Recruit **five** adults who have used a shared workshop, tool library, makerspace or equipment rental, or who have paid for a rental in another context. Do not recruit the builder or anyone who has seen the prototype.

Record only role category, prior rental experience and whether they own/run a shared space. Do not collect unnecessary personal data. Obtain permission before recording audio or screen.

## Session structure (25 minutes)

### 0:00–3:00 — Context

Read exactly:

> “I’m testing an early product called Whirr Bench. I’m not testing you. Please think aloud. I will not help unless you are stuck for more than a minute.”

Do not explain the product before the comprehension pass.

### 3:00–8:00 — Unmoderated first impression

Open `/` at desktop or phone width. Ask:

1. “What do you think this product does?”
2. “Who do you think it is for?”
3. “What part do you trust least?”
4. “Point to where you would start.”

Record verbatim phrases. Do not correct terminology yet.

### 8:00–15:00 — Bench card and consent

Open `/shelf`, then `/s/obj_reference_fan`.

Tasks:

1. Find the rate, the cap and the object’s calibration state.
2. Explain what the sound tag is and is not.
3. Decide whether you would authorize this cap. Say why.
4. Find what happens to uncertain time.
5. Find how to dispute a bill **before** starting a session.

Observe whether they mistake the sound tag for a security guarantee or the microphone for a lock.

### 15:00–20:00 — Listening Room comprehension

Show `/session/:id` in the consent/microphone-closed state. Do not open a microphone unless the participant explicitly consents and the environment is appropriate.

Ask:

1. “When would the bill start?”
2. “What would make the number stop growing?”
3. “What would you expect to see if the room were too noisy?”
4. “What happens if the tab is hidden?”
5. “If the total looked wrong, what evidence would you ask for?”

Then show the receipt and proof page. Ask:

1. “What does the signature prove?”
2. “What does it not prove?”
3. “Could you verify this without the Whirr server?”

### 20:00–23:00 — Comprehension scoring

Score each answer **independently** as:

- **Clear** — correct and uses the product’s language.
- **Partial** — correct idea, wrong confidence or missing the boundary.
- **Wrong** — believes a stronger claim than the system supports.
- **Not found** — could not locate the answer.

Critical errors:

- believes Whirr physically locks or secures the tool;
- believes the sound tag proves identity or physical presence;
- believes uncertain time is billed;
- believes the proof verifies PayPal offline;
- cannot find the cap before authorizing.

### 23:00–25:00 — Debrief

Ask:

1. “What would you call this category?”
2. “Would you use it at a real bench? Why or why not?”
3. “What would make you distrust the owner or the meter?”
4. “What would you remove from this page?”
5. “What is the one sentence you would tell a friend?”

## Analysis

Report counts, not vibes:

- 5/5 or 0/5 for each comprehension question;
- critical-error count;
- time to find rate/cap;
- terms participants invented that were not in the UI;
- direct quotes with consent.

Do not average a “SUS score” from five people without saying it is exploratory. Do not call the result validation if the sample is convenience-only.

## Changes to test in the next round

Hypotheses, not findings:

- “running minute” may be clearer than “billable minute”;
- the sound tag needs a one-line “not a fingerprint” note on the shelf card;
- the cap should remain visually adjacent to the authorize button;
- the microphone-closed state must say that no money is moving;
- “sound tag match” needs a visible free-time consequence, not just a percentage.
